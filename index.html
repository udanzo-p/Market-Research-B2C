What the research says

    Which segments are most receptive to polymer bathware, and why

    SegmentReceptivityWhy
    Mid-price new-build apartments (₹40 L–2.5 Cr), Tier 1–2HighFittings are specified, not shopped; specs are mostly "or equivalent"; buyer judges finish in a sample flat, not material; brass inflation hurts the developer's BOQ
    Young nuclear families and nest-buildersHighAesthetics and colour matter most (Gen Z 35% self-expression); first fit-out is a package purchase; price-sensitive on the second and third bathroom
    Replacement in hard-water and coastal citiesHighPolymer's corrosion and scale resistance is a real benefit; plumber-led purchase where a QR reward decides; replacement is 25%+ of faucet demand
    Elderly and assisted households (SAFE)HighLever handles, cut-free edges, anti-scald; buyer is often the adult child; no incumbent owns the "safe bathroom"
    Institutions: hostels, hospitals, PMAY, governmentHighISI-marked, unbranded specs; PTMT already used; BirlaNu relationships exist (SRM, Yashoda, AAI, L&T)
    Landlords and rental turnoverMediumEconomy buy at turnover; HUE inserts fit a cheap refresh; organised co-living (0.3 mn beds) is spec-driven
    Economy to upper-midscale hotels, Tier 2–3MediumOwner buys, lifecycle cost matters, no published supplier; brand standards can block
    Premium upgraders (renovation, ₹1.5 Cr+)MediumArchitect-led; finish and warranty weigh equally with design; polymer needs the cut-away and the proof pack
    Luxury and branded residencesLowIdentity-led buying; Artize, GROHE, Gessi anchors; blank-canvas shells; Atelier is a credential only

    Marketing approach by stakeholder

    What the evidence says about the unit

    • Parents are in the room. 83% of buyers say parents were meaningfully involved; 19% say parents made the final call and 28% had a veto; 44% of married women name in-laws as the primary influencer (Ghar.tv Parents' Veto Index, n=1,247, 2026).
    • Women buy for use. 69% of women buyers are end-users (Anarock 2025); 87% of women end-users are first-timers (Anarock-LIC HFL).
    • Developers pre-specify. Premium developers pre-spec Jaquar Continental or Hindware; buyer upgrades are "one notch up" at ₹1.5–3 lakh a bathroom and go through the developer's fixed materials menu (Studio Matrx). Godrej's customisation FAQ lists no fittings menu.
    • Renovation is professional-led. 97–98% of renovating owners hire a professional: contractors and civil engineers 33%, architects 19%, interior designers 19%; 63% hire a plumber (Houzz India 2018–19).
    • Contractors substitute. Trade guides warn contractors may silently swap generics for specified brands, which is why installed-product QR verification matters.
    • Plumber influence is real but unmeasured in India. Astral, Finolex and Hindware treat the plumber as the primary influencer; Parryware, Prince and Hindware each enrol about one to one and a half lakh. No Indian survey gives a percentage.
    • Luxury buyers want blank canvases. UHNI buyers of branded residences strip and redo bathrooms with their own designers (GRI, 2026).
    First customer interaction

    Be present where the home is chosen, not where the tap is bought

    The six touchpoints in the directions, plus online and the brand centre, against the lifecycle segments. The cell is how much that touchpoint shapes that segment's choice. Below, the asset NuFlow needs at each touchpoint.

    The asset at each touchpoint

    Evidence

    • 80% of Indian home shoppers discover products on social media; 57% of 18–28s rely on creators against 27% of 45–60s (Deloitte Home Reset, Sep 2026).
    • 55% of urban offline home-décor shoppers research online first (Google-Ipsos, n=6,600).
    • Sample flats are built with premium finishes to sell the project; delivered flats can differ, which buyers resent (Housing.com). A sample flat with NuFlow installed is a demonstration to the buyer and a spec to the developer at once.
    • Jaquar runs 22 centres (7 Jaquar World, 15 Orientation Centres); Kohler 3 experience centres plus Studio Kohler Hyderabad; GROHE about 400 points. None publish footfall or conversion.
    • Showrooms and experience centres sell sanitaryware and premium CP; dealers sell mid-range; online sells accessories and standard taps (trade guidance).

    First-interaction plan, FY28

    • Two sample flats in Hyderabad with the Premier kit, plus a "what is behind the wall" cut-away panel for the sales gallery.
    • Developer sales-gallery kit: buyer-facing QR passport card, colour-insert swatch board, one-page warranty.
    • Four architect studio visits with the Product Intelligence pack (the BirlaNu-referenced practices first).
    • 300 fitters trained; QR reward paid only for installs with no 90-day callback.
    • Creator content on the three occasions that convert: first flat set-up, parents' bathroom retrofit, rental refresh with HUE inserts.
    • Experience centre only in Year 2, after POs, in a city where Kohler, Roca and Hindware already court specifiers.
    Institutional · trade · non-trade

    Three channel families, scored on reach, margin and penetration

    The directions ask which channels offer the greatest reach, margins and penetration. Each row is scored 1 to 5 on five criteria; weights re-rank them. Sub-rows break the non-trade opportunity into the customer types the brief lists.

    Reach: share of target demand touched. Margin: NuFlow's realised margin after channel cost. Penetration speed: time to first meaningful revenue. Cost to serve: sales and service effort per rupee (5 = low cost). Polymer receptivity: how the channel's buyers judge a polymer-hybrid.

    A · Institutional

    Developers, contractors, architects, residential project developers, large housing institutions.

    • Bulk pricing from the kit price book (30% ceiling)
    • Specification partnerships and "or equivalent" approvals
    • Builder and contractor relationships through BirlaNu's accounts
    • Sample-flat installations as the demonstration
    • Product demonstrations for procurement and MEP teams

    Launch engine: 47% of Year 1 revenue in the plan. Detail in the B2B explorer.

    B · Retail and trade

    Dealers, distributors, plumbing retailers, bath showrooms, plumbers and installers.

    • Dealer onboarding in clusters of 40; counted only after a reorder
    • Channel margins 25% + 4% hub (studios), 22% + 8% distributor (BirlaNu dealers)
    • Visibility: hero 8 SKUs and a demo unit per studio
    • Training in 25-person sessions; QR rewards for verified installs
    • Incentive selling: reorder bonus, protected pricing, one price everywhere
    • Premium displays for HUE and SENSE

    Growth engine: 60% of Year 5 revenue. Lifecycle segments give each cluster a target household.

    • Residential facility management: replacement contracts for RWAs and co-living operators
    • Renovation and maintenance contractors: package deals on CORE and SAFE

    Global · price tiers, polymer position, compliance, phasing

    India first; then the markets that already tolerate a polymer tap

    Where polymer sits today in each geography, where a finish-led hybrid could credibly move, what certification the product needs, which segment buys, and the phased roadmap. India already exports USD 1.08 bn of taps and valves (HS 8481.80); the question is which of those routes a polymer-hybrid can use.

    India: the price ladder and where polymer sits

    Listed MRPs for a basin mixer or pillar cock, November 2026. PTMT lives below ₹500; NuFlow's ladder runs ₹899 to ₹24,999.

    The gap NuFlow fills is the one incumbents left: a polymer body with a mid-premium finish between Essco and Jaquar Prime. Cera's Polipluz and Truflo went the other way, down into the ₹9,000 Cr unorganised tier.
